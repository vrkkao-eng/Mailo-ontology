Medical AI Legal Ontology (MAILO): Description back to ToC

Version 5.2.0 — second corrective release, 10 September 2026. Continues the audit begun in v5.2.0, which covered the CJEU paragraph citations, the patent and EPO instances, an initial set of legislative citations, and the inclusion and encoding boundaries. This release audits everything that remained: the cross-framework tensions, the principles and cases outside the trilogy, the GDPR, AI Act, MDR, Data Act and Trade Secrets Directive provisions, and the SHACL shapes. Coverage rises from 48 to 71 articles. The recurring defect is not miscitation but misattribution of normative character. Two provisions had their effect inverted and two pairs were transposed. CROSS-FRAMEWORK TENSIONS. None of the ten was sound. Four cited the wrong provision: DGA neutrality is Art.12(1)(a) not Art.10; Data Act trade-secret safeguards are Arts.4(6)-(8) and 5(9)-(11) not Art.15; the EHDS opt-out tension still carried proposal numbering; and the PLD tension attributed the defect presumption to Art.11 rather than Art.10(4). Two cited C-203/22 at paras 60-70 instead of 59-62. Seven instances used conflictType with hasResolutionStatus while three used conflictsWith with involvesFramework, so any cross-tension query silently omitted a third of the set; the model is harmonised. Tension_BlackBox_PatentProtection carries a warning that it conflates patent protection with secrecy — a patent requires disclosure, as T 161/18 and T 1191/19 both demonstrate. GDPR. Art.22(1) had been recorded as a flat prohibition, pre-committing to one side of a contested question C-634/21 did not settle, and omitting profiling. Art.22(3) had been described as applying whenever a derogation applies; it attaches only to points (a) and (c) of Art.22(2), which matters for medical AI running on a national legal basis. Art.22(4) had been called 'enhanced protection' for special category data when it is a prohibition admitting only Art.9(2)(a) or (g) — consequential because health data sits squarely in Art.9(1). Art.15(1)(h) had omitted the existence limb and the cross-reference scoping it to Art.22(1) and (4). AI ACT. Art.86's right to explanation is confined to Annex III systems. Medical devices reach high-risk status by the Annex I route under Art.6(1), so Art.86 does not apply to a diagnostic or imaging AI device, and neither does the Art.26(11) duty to inform affected persons; for that population GDPR Arts.15(1)(h) and 22 carry the explanation duty alone. A HighRiskRoute vocabulary encodes the distinction, and MDR Annex VIII Rule 11 — the classification rule that produces it, previously absent — is linked through triggersRoute. Art.13 addresses DEPLOYERS, not affected persons, which is the pivot of the trade-secret argument. Art.10(3) had dropped the qualifier 'to the best extent possible'. OTHER INSTRUMENTS. TSD Art.5 had been read as disapplying secrecy where information rights are exercised; Art.5(a) is the freedom of expression of Charter Art.11, and the provision that preserves regulatory disclosure duties is Art.1(2)(b), now added along with Art.3 on reverse engineering. PLD Arts.4 and 7 were transposed and Art.5, the strict-liability rule itself, was missing. Data Act Art.3 is a design duty and Art.4 the access right, with different application dates; Art.17 is the request procedure, not public-emergency sharing. Six EHDS provisions were added, of which Art.53(1)(e)(ii) matters most: it expressly permits secondary use for training, testing and evaluating algorithms in medical devices and AI systems, the legal pathway on which medical-AI training under the EHDS rests. EHDS Art.52, governing IP and trade secrets in secondary use, was also absent. CASES AND SHAPES. G 1/19 is a COMVIK inventive-step ruling, and the Enlarged Board held that whether the simulated system is technical is NOT decisive; all three EPO instances were attributed to EPC Art.52 and are re-anchored to Arts.56, 83 and 84. C-307/22 (FT v DW) involves no AI. Four cases are rescoped, leaving two of sixteen with a verified medical-AI technology claim. Correcting the class hierarchy in v5.2.0 inverted what shape target classes mean, since targeting AutomatedDecisionSystem was narrower than MedicalAISystem before and is broader after; three C-817/19 shapes had drifted and all five now target MedicalAISystem. MedicalAIComplianceShape records that its C-817/19 constraints are transposed rather than held, and that its conjunctive structure is a modelling choice: conformance evidences alignment with MAILO's model, not compliance with law. NOT VERIFIED. Three CJEU cases lack ECLIs, deliberately not supplied from memory. Three UPC instances retain unverified substantive assertions, flagged on the instances. No finding in either corrective release has been reviewed by a second EU data-protection specialist.
