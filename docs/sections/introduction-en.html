Introduction back to ToC

Medical AI systems deployed in the EU are subject to overlapping, only partially harmonised obligations across the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the Medical Device Regulation (MDR), the AI Act, the revised Product Liability Directive (PLD), the European Health Data Space Regulation (EHDS), the Data Governance Act (DGA), the Data Act, and the Trade Secrets Directive. Compliance work today is largely manual, relying on legal expertise to interpret how these instruments interact for a given system, with no machine-checkable representation of the resulting obligations.

Existing AI-assisted legal-compliance tools are predominantly built on large language models that generate advice from patterns learned during training. Such tools are useful for retrieval and drafting, but they are not verifiable: they cannot guarantee that a compliance claim follows deterministically from the underlying legal text, and they cannot produce an auditable trace connecting a system's technical documentation to a specific legal obligation.

MAILO's goal is to provide this missing verification layer: a deterministic, auditable knowledge graph that encodes the relevant obligations as OWL 2 classes and SHACL shape constraints, so that a medical AI system's technical documentation can be validated against explicit legal rules and produce a structured, traceable conformance report rather than a generated summary. The ontology is grounded not only in legislative text but in three CJEU judgments interpreting Article 22 GDPR in the context of automated decision-making, ensuring the encoded rules reflect binding judicial interpretation rather than statutory text alone.
